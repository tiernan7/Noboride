# Minimum mechanical-input estimate

The comparison uses supplied masses, elevation and speed under a no-assistance assumption. **Est. min. avg power** in the main table is the time-weighted average of the local windowed minimum-power curve. The detailed panel also reports a separate **Whole-range net bound**, calculated only from endpoint energy:

`Pmin = max(0, [m × 9.80665 × Δh + ½m × (vend² − vstart²)] / elapsedSeconds)`

Gravity and signed momentum remain a breakdown of the whole-range net bound. The two averages differ because clipping negative local windows to zero before time averaging prevents downhill energy changes from cancelling positive windows elsewhere. For example, 200 W followed for equal time by −200 W has a zero net bound but a 100 W average after local clipping. This is a descriptive windowed estimate, not a tighter proven bound derived from exact instantaneous measurements. Equal entry/exit speeds cancel kinetic energy even when neither is zero. Slowing can supply climbing energy; acceleration adds an energy requirement. Zero means no positive lower bound under this model, not zero pedalling or proven coasting. Air resistance, rolling resistance, drivetrain losses and wheel rotational energy are omitted. A rider can pedal while slowing or travelling at constant speed on flat road. These plots cannot diagnose unusually high bike friction. The assumptions exclude motor assistance, towing and propulsive wind. GPS/elevation error can invalidate the nominal bound; this is not measured crank power, physiological effort or FTP.

The energy terms of fuller cycling models are described in [Martin et al., Validation of a Mathematical Model for Road Cycling Power](https://collections.lib.utah.edu/dl_files/b4/8e/b48ef26086091662c561e673d7bd990d77868437.pdf). No resistance coefficients are guessed in this implementation.

## Inputs and persistence

Both weights must be supplied; blank weights disable estimates. Bike weight includes carried equipment as the user chooses. Display units follow Settings, while storage/calculations use kilograms, meters and seconds. Rider mass must be positive and at most 350 kg; bike/equipment mass positive and at most 200 kg. Never infer mass from heart rate or activity speed.

Optional **Assumed net height error ±** and **Assumed endpoint speed error ±** generate a sensitivity range. The former bounds the elevation difference, not each elevation separately; the latter varies both endpoint speeds independently, flooring speeds at zero. Endpoints that minimize/maximize energy define the displayed range, clipped at zero. These are user assumptions, not measured GPS accuracy, statistical confidence limits or a formal outlier test. No default accuracy is fabricated. Both assumptions must be entered for shading; blank inputs leave error unquantified. Supported limits are 0–100 m and 0–20 m/s internally, including explicit zero.

Apply weights saves masses and error assumptions in the current comparison's existing settings JSON. Other comparisons retain their own settings. Backups validate these optional fields; older backups default to null. Failed saves restore calculation settings and retain typed inputs for retry. No SQL migration is required.

## Elevation, speed and continuity

Verified same-road comparisons share the road reference's elevation; manual/independent ranges use each pass's own elevation, with explicit labels. Height is net endpoint change, not accumulated ascent. Each pass retains its own elapsed time, including pauses. Source samples, IDs, boundaries and recorded power remain unchanged.

Speed uses a time-weighted, piecewise-linear average of the normalized speed channel over up to five seconds centered at each endpoint, clipped at range boundaries. The importer can already have populated missing speed from distance/time; this channel is not guaranteed to be an independent sensor measurement. Where the speed channel is incomplete, the estimate derives speed from continuous distance/time over the same interval. At least one second is required.

A deterministic consistency screen leaves speed-dependent estimates unavailable if averaged speed and distance/time differ by more than both 2 m/s and 25% of averaged speed. This screens contradictory channels; it is not a calibrated GPS-error bound or statistical outlier rejection. It cannot detect errors shared by both channels. Source data and other calculations remain intact. In particular, differentiating an abrupt cumulative-distance correction must not become confident acceleration power.

Whole-range estimates require complete elevation and continuous timing/distance support. Missing elevation, explicit breaks, invalid timestamps/decreasing distance or gaps over 30 seconds suppress the affected calculation with a reason. A local profile may still show supported windows when a different part of the range is unavailable. No missing elevation or sensor measurements are filled to draw a curve.

## Local slope and power display

Select one available pass to colour its elevation profile by grade and show a linked minimum-input power trace. Grade is 100 × height change ÷ reference distance over the exact same clipped physical window used for the power calculation; it is not a separate grade-window calculation. Axes offer distance, normalized progress or elapsed time. Hover links the source-map cursor and reports gravity, momentum, the bound, error sensitivity and actual window distance/time. The labelled grade colours match the Ride browser (under 3%, 3–6%, 6–9%, 9–12%, ≥12%); gray means grade unavailable. Power stays in the separate lower trace. A supported grade can remain visible when inconsistent speeds leave power unavailable. A zero-valued bound remains distinct from an unsupported window.

The physical distance window is shared with gradient (10–300 m), clipped at range endpoints. Shared-reference elevation aligns to each pass by normalized distance progress; interior alignment remains approximate, not exact GPS projection. Local energy is divided by the elapsed time between window boundaries, including pauses inside. The display samples 201 temporal stations; these are overlapping windows, not independent measurements or exact instantaneous watts. Their average need not reproduce the endpoint-only net bound. The displayed curve average integrates adjacent supported rows with the trapezoidal rule over elapsed time, includes recorded zero estimates and divides by supported seconds. Missing intervals are never zero-filled or bridged. If any time is unsupported, the table labels the value **Partial** and gives the fraction of elapsed time represented; it is then not a whole-section average. This is time weighting, never a mean over distance or display points. Neither greater display density nor a smaller window increases source accuracy.

The main comparison metric **Estimated minimum power** overlays these same local curves for selected passes, retaining per-pass visibility and date colours. Same-road overlays use approximate distance progress; independent-hill overlays retain progress/distance/elapsed/raw-ascent axes. Hover chooses the nearest actual station, since uniform temporal stations need not be uniformly spaced along distance. Mass/window/section changes recalculate curves and table averages together.

CSV retains `recorded_power_w` and legacy `gravity_only_power_w`/mass/height/basis/status columns, and appends explicitly named minimum-input, kinetic-component, endpoint-speed, assumed-error and sensitivity fields. `minimum_input_power_w` retains the endpoint-only net bound for compatibility. Added `windowed_minimum_average_w`, `windowed_minimum_supported_seconds` and `windowed_minimum_time_coverage` identify the curve average and its support. Estimated watts never fill the recorded-power channel. Numerical tests use controlled synthetic examples; they do not calibrate the user's recordings against a power meter.
