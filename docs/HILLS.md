# Hill identities, summaries and comparisons

A named hill is a user-reviewed physical terrain identity containing recorded source ranges. It is distinct from both a comparison context and one pass through that hill. Comparison sections remain local to their comparison. The Hills tab presents one point per named hill, never one extra hill for every recording.

## Create or extend a hill in Comparisons

Use **Add to hill library** in the active comparison header. Select the whole comparison or just the individual sections to add. Choose a new hill name or an existing hill group. Each new or changed recording requires its own **Review alignment** step before Save is enabled. Reopening an unchanged saved pass retains its previous review.

When a comparison contains known hill members, the action becomes **Edit saved hill**. The form selects the existing group, identifies each saved member and loads its hill-only boundaries, even when the timing comparison has different GPS-trimmed bounds or new entry IDs. Association uses stable observation IDs, original entry IDs and unambiguous source provenance; it does not guess from names or nearby coordinates. Saved reviews survive reload/context changes. Editing one pass clears that pass's approval; changing the target or selected membership requires fresh review. Saving updates the existing member ID and group rather than making another hill.

Review shows the selected range in blue and other recordings in gray, with their original GPS coordinates and start/end markers. A separate elevation/gradient overlay includes about 150 m of approach and aftermath. Its horizontal axis is physical distance from each selected start, and elevations are relative to each start; lengths and end positions are not normalized to force agreement. A passing GPS check explicitly does not certify matching crests. Its Start/End controls edit hill-only boundaries. They do not change the section in the comparison or the imported recording. Confirm the physical road, direction and coverage for each pass. Differing coverage is allowed; equal endpoints are not required. Continuous GPS mapping must accommodate all ranges being reviewed; gaps, reversed direction and substantial divergence require adjustment or exclusion. Passing these geometric checks is not proof that nearby parallel roads are identical: the explicit review still matters.

The end readout reports its gradient and position before/after a nearby recorded high point. **Use nearby recorded high point as end** is an explicit shortcut, not an automatic hill correction: it chooses the highest recorded elevation within about 150 m of the current end, past the minimum 20 m hill span. Missing elevation, explicit breaks or gaps over 30 s within that window disable it. Recorded noise or another nearby ramp can make that point unsuitable; inspect the profile and confirm the changed range. Controls preserve full fractional source-index precision on opening. Neither this shortcut nor profile display changes timing sections or raw recordings.

Saving stores exact fractional hill-only source ranges and a versioned per-pass alignment review, including the original comparison range as provenance. The hill mapping is reconstructed from these saved ranges and original coordinates after reload. Existing compatible membership is reused or replaced rather than adding another copy of the same source interval. Renaming, removing passes and dissolving identities live in the same comparison-side control. These changes never rewrite another comparison's sections or delete imported tracks. Legacy identities without alignment-review metadata remain readable; newly published passes require review in the interface.

## A hill across its recordings

The primary Hills view is a full-width filled hill profile, with one selected recorded feature below it. Gradient uses fixed 3/6/9/12% thresholds. Speed, heart rate and measured power use a labeled blue-to-neutral-to-orange scale over that hill's recorded range. Color opacity is uniform: changing coverage does not masquerade as a sensor change. Missing channels have gray fill and an explicit not-recorded message; valid recorded zero stays zero.

The geographic mapping in `lib/hill-profile.mjs` uses all vetted member ranges, without asking the user to choose a common-endpoint ride. A computational spine starts from the longest eligible recorded geometry and extends through overlapping continuous member ends. Source coordinates are neither translated nor rotated. GPS legs are thinned at roughly 5 m spacing and projected in meters in a local tangent plane. Source-to-spine progress permits up to 15 m backward jitter before enforcing chronological order. A member must cover at least 10 m of forward mapped progress, remain within `max(20 m, min(65 m, 2.5 × gate radius))`, and have no explicit recording breaks or GPS/time gaps exceeding 30 seconds. During review of additions to an existing identity, existing members receive spine priority. This is a display mapper, separate from the conservative same-road timing verifier.

The display samples 101 physical positions along that spine. Different source start/end locations contribute only where they cover a position. Missing sensor/elevation values remain missing. Constant elevation offsets are estimated from overlapping readings using eight damped median iterations, without forcing elevations to agree at either end. Multiple passes within a ride are averaged first; each contributing ride then receives equal weight. The line is the across-ride mean and the band the range of ride means. This is descriptive recording variation, not a statistical confidence interval or surveyed terrain. Hover reports the actual number of rides contributing to the selected channel at that position.

The horizontal axis defaults to percent progress, with mapped distance as an alternative. Mapped span and the spread of source section lengths are reported separately. The **Included recordings** checkboxes, Include all and Exclude all affect only this summary calculation and are remembered locally in the browser. They do not remove membership, alter source sections or filter the **Open all passes in Comparisons** action.

**View summary** from a comparison selects the corresponding named hill and scrolls directly to its graphic. It does not open the different-hill explorer. A compact comparison-side disclosure lists only library passes not already included by provenance or overlapping source interval.

## Different hills stay within Hills

The secondary explorer plots one point per named hill. Terrain and response values first average repeated passes within each ride, then average ride means. Whiskers show the range of ride means. Source boundary differences therefore appear as variation within the same named identity, rather than as invented additional hills. These source-section means are not a surveyed canonical hill length or gain. Filters choose identities containing the selected rides/equipment; they do not silently reduce their membership.

Select shown, Deselect shown, Clear all and drag-box replace/add/remove selection support batches; points also support keyboard activation. Selecting one identity opens its hill profile. Selecting several loads a different-hill graphic directly below the explorer, without creating or modifying any comparison context. The graphic overlays each hill's mapped relative elevation and selected recorded feature against physical distance or percent progress. Its bands and support are descriptive; differing terrain, coverage, interruptions and equipment limit interpretations of speed and heart rate.

## Individual passes stay in Comparisons

**Open all passes in Comparisons** copies every member range into an independent comparison, including recordings excluded from the summary calculation. The first listed pass initializes the explicit timing baseline. Other passes go through existing GPS verification; unavailable matches remain visible for review rather than silently disappearing or inheriting confirmation from their library identity. The ordinary comparison baseline selector remains available.

A populated unnamed current comparison must be named before a new pass context is created. Failure restores the original context and selection. Later library changes do not rewrite copied comparison sections. Imported IDs, filenames, notes and compressed tracks stay unchanged.

Saved independent terrain comparisons from earlier versions remain supported. They expose raw distance, elapsed time, relative-progress and accumulated-ascent traces. Their **Δ elapsed** column is the raw selected-range duration minus the chosen baseline duration; differing boundaries and lengths prevent interpreting it as a same-road advantage. GPS time-delta overlays and common-road terrain attribution remain unavailable for those independent efforts. Raw accumulated ascent is noise sensitive and stops at missing elevation or a discontinuity.

## Persistence and limits

The additive `hill_library` JSON table stores identities, reviewed source snapshots and optional alignment metadata. Existing databases are backed up with SQLite `VACUUM INTO` before creating the table. Workspace backups remain schema version 1 with an optional `hillLibrary` field. Restore validates source ranges, review metadata, identity membership and names transactionally, and accepts older backups. Deleting a ride prunes its hill memberships; comparison snapshots retain provenance independently.

The local server caches derived detector observations and rebuilds after imports, rename/note changes, deletion, library writes and restore. Whole-hill detection remains terrain-only: at least 10 m net gain over 80 m and 1% average grade, split after a 3 m dip; breaks and missing elevation split detection. Detector observations supply suggestions, while published library ranges retain their reviewed boundaries.

The composite mapper is heuristic. Loops, forks, parallel roads, sparse GPS, altitude drift, mismatched distance channels and changing coverage can distort a summary or require manual review. Its computational spine is not a newly inferred official road. Do not use it to loosen same-road timing checks or claim exact geographic time attribution. Large libraries may consume significant time and memory; tracks are loaded on demand. There are no external map tiles, cloud services or inferred physiological/intent labels. Concurrent editing between browser windows is not coordinated.
