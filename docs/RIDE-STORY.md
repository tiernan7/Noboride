# Ride story, nested terrain and route review

This implementation includes the three original ride-story foundations, the main hill-library and multi-channel comparison workflows, and a minimum mechanical-input foundation. It is interactive post-ride analysis of imported recordings. It does not add a live feed, psychological labels, physiological thresholds or an external assistant. The [current roadmap](ROADMAP.md) distinguishes implemented work, unfinished original scope and new proposals. The separate [minimum mechanical-input calculation](GRAVITY-POWER.md) uses supplied weights, elevation and speed/time; full crank-power modeling remains future work.

## Inspect an effort in context

The **Ride browser** is the single source editor. Ride tabs, one local map, detected/manual terrain, linked traces, saved comparison stretches, shared Start/End controls and one Add/Save action share one selected source range. It stacks elevation, gradient, speed and heart rate, plus cadence and recorded power when those channels occur in the recording. Each lane has its own labeled scale; a shared cursor selects one original normalized source sample across all lanes and the map. Hover, touch, or use the keyboard-accessible cursor slider. Drag horizontally across any trace to select a range; a tap moves the cursor, and vertical touch scrolling remains available. The axes can show distance or elapsed recording time, including pauses. Original source indices, timestamps and coordinates are unchanged.

**Show full ride** is selected by default. Uncheck it to retain 500 m before and after the selection, bounded by available data; choose 250 m, 1 km or 2 km when needed. The context selector is disabled while the full recording is shown. Selecting a hill or passage preserves the chosen view mode. **Select full ride** changes selection boundaries; **Show full ride** changes only the view. Neither crops the underlying recording nor adds comparison entries. The approach summary under Selection surroundings and data notes reports observed positive elevation changes and recording interruptions within the displayed approach. Positive elevation changes are noise-sensitive, not a surveyed ascent total.

Comparison sections appear together in the tree and as numbered rows beneath the traces using the current distance/time axis. Click a row or press Enter/Space to edit that same section without a forced page jump. Selecting an included terrain item keeps its accepted-entry identity rather than creating another range. **Add to comparison** creates a section and its entry together; **Save changes** updates its name, type, boundaries and included range together in one comparison-workspace write. Raw selection of a uniquely overlapping existing range reopens it for editing; use New section or Select another stretch for a separate range. In context view, only included ranges intersecting the displayed window appear on the map/chart rows. Overlapping map ranges retain their full source geometry. The map refits when its displayed context or container size changes, not with every selection/cursor update.

Traces retain source samples rather than averaging effort into categories. Missing values, explicit breaks and analysis-gap intervals break lines. Gradient is calculated using the existing physical grade window, not inferred from pixels. Speed can be recorded or derived from distance/time by the existing importer; missing HR, cadence and power are never invented. Small display-domain floors avoid magnifying numerical noise into apparent effort changes (1 m elevation, 1 percentage point grade, 0.5 m/s speed, 5 bpm HR, 5 rpm cadence and 10 W recorded power, converted at display). These affect axes, not samples or statistics. Missing optional sensor channels are omitted; missing core channels say Not recorded.

## Terrain hierarchy and corrections

`lib/ride-story.mjs` detects terrain using elevation and source distance, without speed, HR, cadence, power, activity date or bike labels:

- Hills run from an observed low point to a subsequent peak and split after a 3 m drop. Minimum net gain is 10 m over 80 m, with at least 1% average gradient. Level ground at the initial minimum stays in the approach rather than extending a hill backward.
- Ramps sustain at least 6% windowed gradient for 50 m, using a fixed 50 m grade window. They can stand alone below the whole-hill gain threshold. This means sustained *windowed* gradient, not proof that every raw meter has that slope.
- Ascending sequences of hills form a passage when intervening dips are at most 15 m deep and hills are at most 500 m apart. A subsequent peak may be no more than 2 m lower than the previous one. Dip depth and separation are adjustable preview controls under Climb detection assumptions.
- Missing elevation, explicit breaks or observation intervals longer than 30 s split detection and passage joining. No passage bridges an unsupported gap. Hierarchy derives from actual range containment; corrections can change which hill or passage encloses a ramp.

These are inspectable heuristics, not official Garmin climb categories or proof of one riding intention. Altitude drift, sparse sampling and noise can still change suggestions. A staircase-shaped synthetic fixture verifies three hills nested in one passage; this does not establish detection quality on the user's October long ride.

Suggested and custom sections use the same editor. Choose a suggested terrain item or **New section**, name it, choose Section (any terrain), Passage, Hill or Ramp, and adjust the shared Start/End controls. **Add to comparison** accepts it once; subsequent edits use **Save changes**. Cancel discards a new unsaved section. Names and boundaries are local to this comparison, even if another comparison uses the same recording or detector ID. Restore suggestion reverts a detected section to its detector bounds/name within this comparison. It never changes the original samples.

The comparison's optional `sections` array owns these definitions, keyed by ride ID and section ID. Included entries carry `sectionId`; API validation requires a matching, non-deleted definition with identical endpoints and permits only one included entry per section identity. Fractional GPS endpoints are retained until boundaries are explicitly changed. A section edit and its entry save in the same SQLite comparison-workspace row; failed saves restore the prior in-memory workspace and retain the unsaved editor draft for retry.

New sections of the same type with at least 90% recording-distance intersection/union reuse the existing identity; identical endpoints also reuse it when changing terrain type. A stored correction also suppresses an equivalent fresh detector proposal of the same type at that overlap, never another explicit saved definition. Legacy entries without links are associated by their source interval at 60% overlap; ordinary unlinked range editing uses 90% overlap. Both are separate from the configurable suggestion-visibility threshold; this is editor identity assistance, not verification that two roads match. Different recording laps do not overlap in source distance even when their map geometry coincides. Ambiguous old versions remain inspectable; choose one to edit and remove unwanted versions explicitly. Existing copies are not silently discarded.

Nesting recalculates after each saved edit: ramps belong to the smallest enclosing hill/section when one exists, otherwise an enclosing passage; hills and generic sections belong to an enclosing passage. Passage is the top level. Equal-type sections stay separate, and partial overlap never implies containment. Adding a passage can group existing hills automatically. Removing a parent retains other independently included children. Manual choices do not need to pass the detector's terrain thresholds and do not prove same-road identity.

**Delete section from comparison** removes its included versions and retains a `deleted` marker to suppress that suggestion here. Removing a single included version retains other old versions; removing the final version suppresses that saved definition here. Other comparisons and the imported activity remain unchanged. The comparison's original GPS road anchors remain independent of manual boundary corrections, preserving the distinction between proposals verified against those anchors and explicitly edited efforts.

Limits are 1000 stored definitions/deletion markers and 200 included entries per comparison, names up to 120 characters and ranges of at least 20 m. Definitions may use fractional original sample positions. Names, bounds, links and deletion markers travel in workspace backups; no SQL migration was required. Older contexts without `sections` receive a one-time comparison-local copy of relevant legacy activity `climbEdits`. That legacy metadata stays untouched. Fresh comparisons start with `sections: []` and do not inherit those global corrections. Restore merges comparison IDs as before and preserves an unnamed context that contains section definitions.

## Review whether two ranges are the same road

Suggestions remain the existing conservative endpoint/direction/ordered-shape candidates. **Inspect roads** opens a local Leaflet dialog showing the reference in blue and candidate in orange at their original coordinates. Start/end markers and initial-direction arrows preserve recording order. Metrics show raw endpoint distances, initial headings, and 33 ordered distance-progress shape probes. Divergence connectors and the five largest comparable offsets can be inspected at close zoom. Initial bearing is not a direction check at every switchback. Interior progress probes are approximate and are not an exact road-network projection. Missing GPS is not filled for inspection; discontinuities disable comparable probes.

No track is translated or rotated to force agreement. A parallel road may pass broad GPS tolerances, so the output is a proposal requiring judgement. This foundation does not automatically assign a global hill/road identity from proximity. A dedicated synthetic pair 20 m apart verifies visible offsets and persisted rejection without modifying coordinates.

- Confirm same road records that judgement. An automatic candidate retains `kind: matched` and is still reverified under current settings on use. The quick Add match action is also an explicit same-road confirmation.
- Inspect road pairing lets an independently selected range be reviewed even when automatic matching failed. Manually confirming it preserves `kind: effort`, source boundaries and a same-road label. It does not fabricate automatic verification; terrain attribution stays disabled for manual corrections.
- Compare similar terrain records a separate manual-effort relationship, with no geographic terrain attribution or equal-effort assumption.
- Reject pairing remembers the range and optional reason. Rejections are scoped to the active comparison's road definition; highly overlapping proposals from that same recording pass stay suppressed. Other laps remain independent. Review again can reverse the decision.
- Rejecting an accepted pairing retains its entry but excludes it from analysis. A rejected saved baseline is preserved and explained; no new baseline is silently substituted. Reconfirm or explicitly choose another.

Accepted entries retain optional `relation`, `review` and `reviewReason`; rejected ranges/reasons use the comparison's optional `matchDecisions`. Rejected decisions reset when all entries/its road definition are removed. Deleting an otherwise unused ride cleans decisions referencing it. Validation rejects automatic GPS entries labeled similar-terrain. Failed review saves restore the previous in-memory context and leave the dialog open with an error. CSV includes road relationship and review state; rejected rows are excluded.

No SQL schema migration was required: these are optional JSON metadata fields in existing tables. Track compression, import IDs and source data remain unchanged. Older backups and entries remain compatible. Human confirmation records a correction, not a statistical confidence level or proof of road identity.
