      Climb detection assumptions

      Terrain suggestions use elevation only: hills need at least 1% average gradient and 10 m net gain over 80 m, separated by 3 m dips. Ramps sustain at least 6% over 50 m. A fixed 50 m gradient window identifies ramps. Missing elevation and recording breaks separate passages. These are editable suggestions, not official climb categories.

      03 / EXPLAIN THE TIME DIFFERENCE

      Climbs, descents, or somewhere else?

      One reference elevation profile defines terrain for both passes.

      Approximate attribution: tracks are aligned by distance progress between matching GPS endpoints. Internal boundaries may shift with sensor/GPS distance errors. All categories add to elapsed time. Heart rate alone does not establish equal effort.

      Inspect shape discrepancies

      Confirmation records your judgement, not GPS certainty. Manual corrections retain their own source boundaries and bypass automatic checks. Same-road terrain attribution remains unavailable for manually selected or similar-terrain efforts.

      A better answer to “where did I get faster?”

      1. Import original activity files. FIT preserves the most sensors; TCX and timed GPX also work. ZIPs can contain nested activity archives. Activity-list CSV and summary JSON cannot reconstruct a route.
      2. Choose your comparison and source rides. Click the comparison name to rename it. Include rides to browse their tabs; switching tabs does not replace accepted comparison stretches.
      3. Mark the road. Drag across the profile, adjust the sliders or enter GPS coordinates. Add your selection to the active comparison, then preview and accept suggested matching stretches.
      4. Inspect each pass. Repeated laps become separate entries, including several from one ride. Edit opens the same stretch editor; Update stretch applies changes to that entry only. Different roads can be added as manually paired efforts; these do not receive same-road terrain attribution. Comparisons save locally.
      5. Explain the difference. Compare elapsed/moving time, HR, cadence and power where recorded. Adjust the grade window to expose short ramps or reduce noise.

      Record gearing, standing effort, wind, tires, brake drag and saddle changes in ride notes. These cannot be inferred reliably from GPS or HR.

      Data stays in the local SQLite database. Maps are drawn locally from your ride data, with no online map tiles. Your GPS tracks are included in workspace backups.
